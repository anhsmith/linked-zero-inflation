## The linked zero-inflated model of Smith et al. (2012)

- This vignette provides code for fitting the linked zero-inflated model of Smith et al. (2012), in which the probability of an excess zero is a logistic function of the log of the mean count, in brms and Stan, in place of the OpenBUGS code published with the original papers.
  - logit(π) = γ0 + γ1 log(λ); λ is the mean of the count distribution, π the probability of an excess zero.
  - Smith et al. (2012, 2014) supplied R and OpenBUGS code that wrote the likelihood out explicitly (the 'zeros trick'). OpenBUGS is no longer developed [verify: last release 3.2.3, 2014?].
  - In brms, the model needs only the built-in zero-inflated negative binomial family and a non-linear formula, with no custom family or Stan code.
  - `posterior_epred()`, `posterior_predict()`, `loo()`, and `pp_check()` work unchanged.

- Smith et al. (2014) applied the linked model to counts of legal-sized snapper from 1,045 baited underwater video deployments in and around three marine reserves in north-eastern New Zealand, where it was the form of zero inflation selected by DIC.
  - Leigh, Tawharanui, and Te Whanganui-A-Hei (Hahei); 1997–2010; reserve, season, location, area, and year, with interactions.
  - For sublegal snapper, DIC selected a separate linear predictor for π instead.
  - The example here is the Hahei subset of those data, which is also the dataset analysed by Smith et al. (2012).

- Zero-inflated models for counts of organisms add to a count distribution a probability, π, that an observation is an excess zero, and π is usually modelled either as a constant or with a linear predictor of its own.
  - Lambert (1992); the overall mean is (1 − π)λ.
  - Constant π ignores any relation between π and λ; a separate predictor doubles the parameters and splits each effect between two predictors, which Smith et al. (2014) found made the effects for sublegal snapper difficult to interpret.

- For many species, occupancy rises with mean abundance, so a model in which the probability of an excess zero falls as the mean count rises can be both more parsimonious and more realistic than either alternative.
  - Intraspecific occupancy–abundance relationship (Brown 1984).
  - The link adds two parameters, γ0 and γ1, whatever the number of predictors for λ.

- Anderson et al. (2022) named the zero-inflated negative binomial with this link ZINBL and implemented it, by maximum likelihood, in the R package senlm.
  - In the NOAA groundfish data, 91 of the 101 species best modelled with zero inflation (AICc) took a linked form.
  - Rabel (2023) implemented a Bayesian ZINBL with a non-linear mean along a single gradient in the R package modskurt, in Stan.

## Specification in brms

- The linear predictor for the log mean is declared as a non-linear parameter, η, which then enters both the formula for the mean and the formula for zi.
  - `bf(y ~ eta, nlf(zi ~ g0 + g1 * eta), eta ~ ..., g0 ~ 1, g1 ~ 1, nl = TRUE)`.
  - The family supplies the log link for the mean and the logit link for zi, so the formula is written on the scale of the two linear predictors.

- The Stan code generated by brms shows that the specification is exact: zi for each observation is computed from the same η as the mean.
  - Show the two lines from `stancode()`: `mu[n] = nlp_eta[n]` and `zi[n] = nlp_g0[n] + nlp_g1[n] * nlp_eta[n]`.
  - Random effects in η therefore enter π as well, as in Smith et al. (2012).

- A non-linear model in brms requires a proper prior on every non-linear parameter, so the coefficients of η, γ0, and γ1 each need one.
  - Normal(0, 2.5) on all three; Student-t(3, 0, 2.5) on standard deviations (brms default); Gamma(0.01, 0.01) on the shape (brms default).
  - Rescaling the counts shifts log(λ), and so changes γ0 but not γ1.

## Snapper in and around Te Whanganui-A-Hei (Hahei) Marine Reserve

- The example data are counts of legal-sized snapper, Pagrus auratus (Sparidae), from 348 baited underwater video (BUV) deployments made inside and outside Te Whanganui-A-Hei (Hahei) Marine Reserve between 1997 and 2010.
  - Count = maximum number of legal-sized (> 27 cm fork length) snapper in any one frame of a 30 min deployment (MaxN), a measure of relative density.
  - 6 areas (3 inside, 3 outside), 9 years, 2 seasons (spring, autumn); unbalanced.

- Zeros made up 55% of the counts, 83% outside the reserve against 28% inside, so the proportion of zeros fell as the mean count rose.
  - 191 of 348 zeros; mean count 0.32 outside, 3.39 inside.

- The model for the log mean included reserve status and season as fixed effects and area and year as random effects, following Smith et al. (2012).
  - `eta ~ reserve + season + (1 | area) + (1 | year)`.
  - 4 chains × 2,000 post-warmup draws, `adapt_delta = 0.95`; max R̂ 1.004, 2 divergent transitions of 8,000.

- The posterior predictive distribution reproduced the number of zeros, with a median of 190 (95% interval 170 to 210) against 191 observed.

## Comparison with negative binomial and constant zero-inflation models

- Expected log predictive density (PSIS-LOO) was greatest for the linked model, exceeding that of a zero-inflated model with constant π by 6.7 (SE 4.2) and that of a negative binomial model without zero inflation by 7.2 (SE 4.5).
  - Same predictors for λ and same priors in all three; `loo_compare()`; all Pareto k < 0.7.
  - Support is moderate: each difference is less than twice its standard error.
  - NB fit: 4 divergent transitions of 8,000; constant-π fit: none.

- The constant-π model estimated a small probability of an excess zero, 0.11 (0.01 to 0.22), and, like the negative binomial model, predicted fewer zeros than were observed.
  - Predicted zeros, median (95% interval), against 191 observed: NB 180 (160 to 200); constant π 183 (162 to 204); linked 190 (170 to 210).
  - A single π must hold across both reserve statuses, where the observed share of zeros was 83% outside and 28% inside.

## Estimated link between zero inflation and the mean count

- The probability of an excess zero decreased with the mean count, with γ1 = −1.53 (95% credible interval −2.51 to −0.76).
  - γ0 = 0.29 (−0.88 to 1.54).
  - Figure: π against λ, posterior median curve and draws, rug of fitted λ.

- Averaged over the sampled combinations of area, year, and season, the probability of an excess zero was 0.68 (0.49 to 0.79) outside the reserve and 0.19 (0.10 to 0.27) inside.

## Reserve effect

- The mean count is (1 − π)λ, which `posterior_epred()` returns with π computed from the link, so a reserve effect on the scale of the counts can be computed directly from the fitted model.
  - Checked: `posterior_epred()` matched (1 − π)λ computed by hand to a relative difference of 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁴.

- Averaged over the sampled combinations of area, year, and season, mean counts were 11.1 (7.6 to 16.8) times greater inside the reserve than outside.
  - Equal weight per sampled cell; 36 cells inside, 36 outside.
  - Applies to these areas and years, not to a new area or year.

- This estimate is smaller than the 16.4 reported by Smith et al. (2012) and the 16.0 reported for Hahei by Smith et al. (2014), because both evaluated (1 − π)λ at zero area, year, and season effects rather than averaging (1 − π)λ over them.
  - Under the linked model, (1 − π)λ is a non-linear function of η, so the mean of the function differs from the function of the mean.
  - Without the link, the area and year terms would cancel in the ratio; with it, they do not.
  - The three-location model refitted in brms gave 11.1 (7.7 to 16.4) for Hahei as well.
